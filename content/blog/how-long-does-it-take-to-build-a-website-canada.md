<p>One of the most common questions we get from Canadian business owners is: "How long will my website take?" It's also one of the questions most agencies answer vaguely — "6 to 12 weeks," "it depends," "we'll let you know after discovery." That's frustrating when you have a launch deadline or a seasonal campaign on the line.</p>

<p>This guide gives you a realistic breakdown — by website type, agency size, and the factors that actually affect timelines. No padding. No upsell disguised as complexity.</p>

<h2>Typical Canadian Website Build Timelines in 2026</h2>

<p>Here's what to expect based on project scope:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>5-page business website:</strong> 2–3 weeks. Homepage, About, Services, Blog, Contact — this is the most common scope for Canadian small businesses. With a clear brief and fast content approvals, it can ship in under 2 weeks.</li>
  <li><strong>10–15 page professional website:</strong> 3–5 weeks. Adds service sub-pages, team pages, case studies, or location pages. More content to develop and review.</li>
  <li><strong>Ecommerce website (up to 50 products):</strong> 4–8 weeks. Shopify or WooCommerce builds with product setup, payment integration, and shipping configuration.</li>
  <li><strong>Ecommerce website (100+ products):</strong> 8–16 weeks. Large catalogue builds, custom filters, inventory sync, and migration from existing platforms.</li>
  <li><strong>Custom web application or platform:</strong> 3–9 months. Anything with user accounts, dashboards, APIs, or business logic beyond a standard CMS.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What Actually Slows Down a Website Build</h2>

<p>In 10+ years building websites for Canadian businesses, the biggest causes of delay are almost never on the agency side. They're on the client side — and they're all avoidable:</p>

<h3>1. Content not ready at kickoff</h3>
<p>This is the #1 timeline killer. If you don't have written copy, photos, and logos ready when design starts, the project will stall. Agencies can write content for you (we do), but even then, you need to provide input, approve drafts, and supply brand assets before anything can be finalized.</p>

<h3>2. Slow feedback and approval cycles</h3>
<p>Every round of revisions that takes 5 business days instead of 1–2 adds weeks to your timeline. The fastest-launching clients treat their website like a real project with real deadlines — not something to review "whenever I get a minute."</p>

<h3>3. Scope changes mid-build</h3>
<p>"Can we add a booking system?" mid-way through a 5-page build is a scope change — it pushes timelines significantly. Define scope clearly before development starts and hold the line until launch. Add-ons can always come in phase 2.</p>

<h3>4. Multiple decision-makers without a single point of contact</h3>
<p>When 3 partners all have different opinions on the homepage hero, and those opinions arrive piecemeal over 2 weeks, a 5-day task becomes a 3-week standoff. Appoint one internal decision-maker who can consolidate feedback and give a single go/no-go.</p>

<h2>How to Speed Up Your Website Build</h2>

<p>If you want to minimize time to launch, do these things before you hire an agency:</p>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Write a one-page brief</strong> — your goals, target audience, key pages, and any competitor sites you like. An agency that gets a clear brief will move 2x faster than one decoding a vague request.</li>
  <li><strong>Gather content upfront</strong> — your logo in vector format (AI or SVG), professional photos, written copy for key pages, and any branding guidelines. Even rough drafts are better than nothing.</li>
  <li><strong>Commit to 48-hour review turnarounds</strong> — tell your agency you'll provide feedback within 2 business days of every submission. Then do it.</li>
  <li><strong>Name a single point of contact</strong> — one person who can approve design decisions without committee consensus.</li>
  <li><strong>Have your domain and hosting ready</strong> — waiting on domain transfers or DNS propagation at the end of a build adds unnecessary delays.</li>
</ol>

<h2>What Happens If the Agency Goes Quiet?</h2>

<p>A good agency will have a defined communication cadence — weekly status updates, a shared project tracker, and a direct line to your project manager. If an agency goes quiet for more than a week without explanation, that's a red flag. Before you hire, ask how often you'll receive updates and through what channel.</p>
