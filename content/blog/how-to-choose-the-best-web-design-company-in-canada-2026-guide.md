<h2 class="wp-block-heading">3. Red Flags to Watch Out For</h2>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Templates Sold as Custom Design</h3>

<p>Many Canadian web agencies purchase WordPress or Webflow themes and sell them as "custom" websites. A template-based website looks similar to dozens of other sites, loads slower due to bloated code, and ranks poorly because it lacks the technical SEO architecture of a truly custom build.</p>

<p>Ask to see the actual code or ask directly: "Are you building this from scratch or using a theme?" A reputable agency answers honestly.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Outsourcing Overseas Without Disclosure</h3>

<p>Some Canadian web agencies take your project, outsource it to developers in lower-cost countries, and act as project managers. This often results in communication delays, quality inconsistencies, and difficulty making post-launch changes. Ask your agency: "Who will actually be building my website?"</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">No Guarantee or Exit Clause</h3>

<p>Be cautious of agencies that lock you into long-term contracts with no performance clause. The best Canadian web design agencies back their work with a results guarantee — if your site doesn't perform within the agreed timeline, they keep working at no charge.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Vague Timelines and Deliverables</h3>

<p>A professional agency provides a clear project timeline with milestones: discovery, design approval, development, QA, and launch. If an agency can't give you a concrete timeline, they're likely juggling too many projects or lack the process to deliver consistently.</p>
