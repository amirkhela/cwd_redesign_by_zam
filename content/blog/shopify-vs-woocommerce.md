<p>Choose Shopify if you want to sell quickly and let the platform handle hosting, security and checkout. Choose WooCommerce if you already run WordPress, want full control over your store, or expect transaction and app fees to add up as you grow. Both handle GST/HST, Canadian payment processing and shipping across Canada.</p>

<p>We build stores on both. Here is how we decide which to recommend.</p>

<h2>When Shopify is the better choice</h2>

<ul>
<li>You want to launch fast and not think about servers, updates or security patches.</li>
<li>Your team will run the store day to day and needs a simple admin.</li>
<li>You sell on several channels (in person, social media, marketplaces) and want them in one place.</li>
<li>You are comfortable paying a monthly plan plus apps in return for less upkeep.</li>
</ul>

<h2>When WooCommerce is the better choice</h2>

<ul>
<li>Your website already runs on WordPress.</li>
<li>Content matters to your sales: guides, recipes, how-tos, a blog that brings in search traffic.</li>
<li>You need custom rules: wholesale pricing, unusual product options, membership or booking.</li>
<li>You want to avoid platform transaction fees and keep monthly costs tied to hosting.</li>
</ul>

<p>The trade-off is maintenance. WooCommerce stores need plugin updates, backups and security checks. That work is real, and it is what our <a href="/maintenance">maintenance plans</a> cover.</p>

<h2>Shopify vs WordPress: is it the same question?</h2>

<p>Mostly, yes. WordPress on its own is a website platform, not a store. When people compare Shopify with WordPress for selling online, they are really comparing Shopify with WordPress plus WooCommerce. If you only need a few products or a "buy now" button on an otherwise normal website, a simpler option such as a payment link may be enough, and our <a href="/services/wordpress-website-design">WordPress website design</a> page covers what a standard WordPress build includes.</p>

<h2>Canadian specifics</h2>

<p><strong>Sales tax.</strong> Both platforms can charge GST, HST, PST and QST by province. The setup needs care: rates differ by province, and some products are taxed differently. Check it with your accountant before launch.</p>

<p><strong>Payments.</strong> Both support Canadian payment processors, including Stripe, PayPal and Square. Shopify also has its own Shopify Payments.</p>

<p><strong>Shipping.</strong> Both connect to Canada Post and other carriers for live rates and labels.</p>

<p><strong>Currency and language.</strong> Both can sell in Canadian dollars and run in French and English. If you sell in Quebec, plan the French version from the start.</p>

<h2>SEO</h2>

<p>Both can rank well. Shopify fixes some URL patterns (for example /products/ and /collections/) that you cannot change. WooCommerce gives full control over URLs, schema and content. If organic search is your main sales channel and you will publish a lot of content, WooCommerce has the edge. If you run Shopify, our <a href="/blog/shopify-seo-checklist">Shopify SEO checklist</a> covers what to fix first.</p>

<h2>Switching later</h2>

<p>You can move either way. Products, customers and orders can be exported and imported, but the design is rebuilt and every old URL needs a redirect. We cover the steps in <a href="/blog/how-to-migrate-from-shopify-to-woocommerce">moving from Shopify to WooCommerce</a>.</p>

<h2>Shopify or WooCommerce: our recommendation</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>New store, small team, wants simplicity:</strong> Shopify.</li>
<li><strong>Existing WordPress site, content-led, or custom rules:</strong> WooCommerce.</li>
<li><strong>Not sure:</strong> write down who will run the store every day and what they are comfortable doing. That usually decides it.</li>
</ul>
