<p>Having a website and having a website that shows up on Google are two very different things. Thousands of Canadian businesses have websites that Google has never indexed — meaning they're invisible to every potential customer who searches for their services. This guide walks you through exactly what to do to get your Canadian business website found on Google, step by step.</p>

<h2>Step 1: Submit Your Website to Google Search Console</h2>

<p>Google Search Console (GSC) is the free tool Google provides to monitor and manage your site's presence in search results. Start here:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Go to <strong>search.google.com/search-console</strong> and sign in with your Google account</li>
  <li>Add your website as a property (use the URL-prefix method for simplicity)</li>
  <li>Verify ownership by adding a meta tag to your site's &lt;head&gt; or by uploading a verification file</li>
  <li>Once verified, go to <strong>Sitemaps</strong> and submit your sitemap URL (usually <code>yourdomain.ca/sitemap.xml</code>)</li>
</ol>

<p>Submitting your sitemap tells Google which pages exist on your site and how to find them. Without this step, Google may take weeks or months to discover your pages — or miss them entirely.</p>

<h2>Step 2: Request Indexing for Your Key Pages</h2>

<p>Once you're in GSC, use the <strong>URL Inspection tool</strong> to manually request indexing for your most important pages:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Paste your homepage URL into the URL Inspection bar</li>
  <li>Click <strong>Request Indexing</strong></li>
  <li>Repeat for your key service pages, about page, and contact page</li>
</ol>

<p>This doesn't guarantee Google will index the page immediately, but it puts it in the crawl queue and typically results in indexing within 1–7 days for new sites.</p>

<h2>Step 3: Set Up Google Business Profile</h2>

<p>For local Canadian businesses, Google Business Profile (GBP) is as important as your website. It's what powers the Google Maps pack — the 3 results that appear in a box above organic search results for searches like "web design Toronto" or "plumber near me."</p>

<ul>
  <li>Go to <strong>business.google.com</strong> and create or claim your listing</li>
  <li>Choose the correct business category — this is the single most important GBP decision you'll make</li>
  <li>Add your complete address, phone number, hours, and website URL — consistency with your website's NAP (Name, Address, Phone) is critical</li>
  <li>Upload at least 10 high-quality photos: exterior, interior, products, team, and logo</li>
  <li>Ask your first 10 customers for Google reviews — even 5 reviews puts you ahead of most local competitors</li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 4: Fix the Technical SEO Basics</h2>

<p>Google can't rank a site it can't properly read. These technical basics need to be correct before any other SEO effort will work:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Title tags:</strong> Every page needs a unique title tag under 60 characters that includes your target keyword. Example: "Web Design Toronto | From $1,499 | Canadian Web Designs"</li>
  <li><strong>Meta descriptions:</strong> Unique, 120–155 character descriptions for every page — these are what appear under your link in search results and directly affect click-through rate</li>
  <li><strong>H1 headings:</strong> One H1 per page, containing your primary keyword</li>
  <li><strong>Mobile responsiveness:</strong> Google indexes the mobile version of your site first. If it doesn't work on phones, your rankings suffer</li>
  <li><strong>Page speed:</strong> A score of 70+ on Google PageSpeed Insights is the minimum for competitive rankings in Canada</li>
  <li><strong>SSL certificate:</strong> Your site must be accessible at <code>https://</code> — no padlock = trust warning = ranking penalty</li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 5: Create Content for Your Target Keywords</h2>

<p>Google ranks pages that best answer what searchers are looking for. For a Canadian business, this means creating pages and blog posts that target the specific keywords your potential customers are typing:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Service pages:</strong> One dedicated page per service you offer, optimized for "[service] + [city]" keywords. A Toronto plumber needs separate pages for "emergency plumber Toronto," "drain cleaning Toronto," and "water heater installation Toronto."</li>
  <li><strong>Location pages:</strong> If you serve multiple cities, create a dedicated page for each city — with unique, city-specific content. Don't just copy-paste the same page with a different city name swapped in.</li>
  <li><strong>Blog content:</strong> Answer the questions your customers are actually asking. "How much does [service] cost in [city]?" "What's the best [product type] for [use case]?" These are goldmines for long-tail keyword traffic.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 6: Build Local Citations</h2>

<p>A local citation is any online mention of your business's Name, Address, and Phone number (NAP). Google uses citations to verify that your business is legitimate and to determine local search rankings. For Canadian businesses, prioritize:</p>

<ul>
  <li>yellowpages.ca</li>
  <li>yelp.ca</li>
  <li>canadianbusiness.com</li>
  <li>canada411.ca</li>
  <li>Industry-specific directories (e.g., houzz.com for contractors, ratemds.com for healthcare)</li>
</ul>

<p>Consistency is critical — your business name, address, and phone number must be identical across all listings and your website.</p>

<h2>How Long Does It Take to Get on Google?</h2>

<p>For brand new Canadian websites:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Getting indexed:</strong> 1–4 weeks after submitting your sitemap</li>
  <li><strong>Appearing in Google Maps:</strong> 2–8 weeks after setting up and verifying your Google Business Profile</li>
  <li><strong>Ranking on page 1 for competitive keywords:</strong> 3–12 months of consistent SEO work, depending on competition</li>
  <li><strong>Ranking for low-competition local keywords</strong> (e.g., "[service] in [smaller Canadian city]"): 4–12 weeks with proper optimization</li>
</ul>

<p>The good news: in most Canadian cities outside Toronto and Vancouver, local keyword competition is low enough that a properly built and optimized website can reach page 1 within 60–90 days.</p>
