<p>Searching "web design companies near me" usually means you want a reliable agency that can build a professional, high-converting website — and with so many options, it's hard to tell a genuinely good one from a template shop with good marketing. Here's an actual checklist to evaluate a company before you sign anything.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-why-local">Why Search Locally in the First Place?</h2>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Better communication:</strong> a nearby team means faster response times and the option of an in-person meeting when something needs a real conversation, not an email thread.</li>
<li><strong>Local market understanding:</strong> a local agency has a practical sense of your competitors and regional customer behavior, not just generic best practices.</li>
<li><strong>Ongoing support:</strong> when you need an update or something breaks, a local team can typically respond faster than an overseas one working across time zones.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-portfolio">Evaluate the Portfolio for Range, Not Just Polish</h2>
<p>Anyone can show you their three best-looking projects. Look instead for range across industries and layouts — that's a sign they can actually build for your specific business rather than adapting the same template every time. Then go check the live sites: do they load fast? Do they work cleanly on mobile? A portfolio screenshot hides both of those problems.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-seo-knowledge">Ask Specific Questions About SEO</h2>
<p>"We'll optimize it for SEO" is not an answer — it's a placeholder for one. Ask what that actually means: clean URL structure, page speed targets, meta tags and schema markup, and a plan for ongoing content. A company that builds <a href="/services/seo">SEO in from the start</a> saves you from paying to fix it later.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-custom-vs-template">Custom Design vs. Template: The Real Trade-off</h2>
<p>A template is faster and cheaper to launch, but it caps how much your site can stand out or scale with custom functionality later. Custom design costs more upfront but gives you full control over branding, performance, and whatever you need to add down the road. Which one is right depends on your stage — a template can be the right call for a very early business, but most companies outgrow it faster than expected.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-reviews">Check Client Reviews — and Read Past the Star Rating</h2>
<p>Testimonials and Google reviews reveal real client satisfaction, but read the actual text, not just the star count. Look for specifics — did the client mention communication, timeline, results — rather than generic five-word praise, which is easier to fabricate or cherry-pick.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-pricing">Demand Pricing Transparency</h2>
<p>A fair quote breaks down what's included: page count, revisions, whether hosting and SEO setup are covered, and what happens after launch. Be cautious of a quote far below market rate — corners cut on hosting, security, or SEO fundamentals tend to cost more to fix later than they saved upfront.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-red-flags">Red Flags to Watch For</h2>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>No visible portfolio of real client work — or a portfolio full of stock template demos.</li>
<li>Vague, hand-wavy answers about SEO with no specifics on structure, speed, or ongoing strategy.</li>
<li>No mention of what happens after launch — maintenance, support, or security updates.</li>
<li>Pressure to sign before you've seen a written scope, timeline, and price.</li>
</ul>
