<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> a construction company website wins estimate requests when it shows real projects sorted by service, proves you are licensed and insured, and makes asking for a quote take less than a minute. Build one page per main service, put your phone number in the header of every page, and keep the quote form short. In Canada, a solid contractor website costs roughly $450 to $1,000 with us, depending on the number of pages.</p>

<p>Below is the checklist we use when we build websites for contractors, renovators and trades.</p>

<h2>The pages every construction website needs</h2>

<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Page</th><th>What it must do</th></tr>
</thead>

<tr><td>Home</td><td>Say what you build and where in one line, show 3 to 6 recent projects, and give a clear "Get a free estimate" button</td></tr>

<h2>Lead with projects, not slogans</h2>

<p>Every contractor's homepage says "quality craftsmanship" and "customer satisfaction". Buyers skip it. What they look for is a project like theirs. If someone is planning a basement renovation, show them basements you have finished, not a mixed gallery of decks, roofs and commercial work.</p>

<p>Before-and-after pairs work best for renovation work. If you do not have professional photos, clear, well-lit phone photos of finished jobs still beat stock images, because visitors want proof the work is yours.</p>

<h2>One page per service</h2>

<p>A single "Services" page that lists framing, drywall, electrical and plumbing as bullet points does little for Google or for visitors. Give each main service its own page with the scope, typical timeline, any permits or certifications involved and matching project photos.</p>

<p>This is also how you show up in search. Someone typing "basement renovation Brampton" should land on your basement page, not your homepage. If you serve several cities, a page for each one, with real projects from that area, works the same way.</p>

<h2>Trust signals construction buyers check</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Licences and memberships:</strong> trade licences, your local home builders' association, manufacturer certifications for roofing, windows or HVAC.</li>
<li><strong>Insurance and WSIB:</strong> state your liability coverage and WSIB (or your province's workers' comp) clearly. Many buyers ask for it before they hire.</li>
<li><strong>Warranty:</strong> say what you guarantee and for how long.</li>
<li><strong>Specific reviews:</strong> "They finished our basement on schedule and the drywall is perfect" persuades far more than a generic five stars. Our guide to <a href="/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews">getting more Google reviews</a> shows how to ask for them.</li>
<li><strong>A real address and team photos:</strong> they show you are an established business.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Make asking for a quote easy</h2>

<p>Keep the quote form to five fields or fewer: name, phone or email, project type, location and a short description. Long forms lose leads. The form starts the conversation; your call qualifies the job.</p>

<p>Put your phone number and a "Get a free estimate" button in the header on every page. Many people search for contractors on their phone, often with an urgent problem, and they will call whoever is easiest to reach.</p>

<p>A short "How it works" section helps too: consultation, written quote, scheduling, completion and walkthrough. It tells first-time buyers what to expect.</p>

<h2>Mistakes that cost contractors leads</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>A stale portfolio.</strong> Projects from three years ago make a busy company look quiet. Add a few new jobs every quarter, and post the same photos to your Google Business Profile.</li>
<li><strong>Hidden contact details.</strong> If someone with a burst pipe has to hunt for your number, they call the next result.</li>
<li><strong>Slow pages.</strong> Construction sites are photo-heavy. Oversized images make a site crawl on mobile data. Compress photos and use modern formats such as WebP.</li>
<li><strong>No service area.</strong> Say which cities and regions you work in, in text, not only in a map image.</li>
</ul>
