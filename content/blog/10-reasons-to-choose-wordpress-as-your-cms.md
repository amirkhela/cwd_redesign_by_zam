<p><strong>43% of every website on the internet runs on WordPress</strong> — and there's a reason Canadian small businesses keep choosing it over Squarespace, Wix, or custom-coded sites. If you're deciding which CMS to build on (or migrate to), this guide gives you 10 concrete reasons WordPress wins — plus the scenarios where it's the wrong call. If you'd rather skip the research and just get a site that ranks and converts, <a href="/contact">book a free consultation with our team</a>.</p>

<p>WordPress is a content management system that revolutionized how small businesses approach web publishing. Its flexible, user-friendly nature, combined with a vast repository of themes and plugins, makes it a strong default choice for anyone building a business website in Toronto or across Canada.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-open-source">1. It's free, open-source software you actually own</h2>
<p>WordPress's open-source core means there's no licensing fee and no vendor lock-in — the software, and everything you build with it, is yours. A global community of developers continuously improves the platform, which is a big part of why it has stayed relevant for over two decades while other CMS platforms have come and gone.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-content-management">2. It handles more than just blogging</h2>
<p>WordPress started as a blogging tool but has grown into a full CMS capable of managing text, images, audio, video, e-commerce catalogs and membership content in one place. That range means most businesses never need a second platform bolted on as they add pages, products, or content types.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-security">3. It has a two-decade security and reliability track record</h2>
<p>Since its launch in 2003, WordPress has shipped regular core updates that patch vulnerabilities as they're discovered. The platform's reputation for security issues usually traces back to outdated plugins or unmanaged installs, not the core software — which is exactly why a <a href="/services/website-maintenance">maintenance plan</a> matters more than the CMS choice itself.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-plugins">4. A plugin for almost anything you need</h2>
<p>Need SEO tools, e-commerce, booking forms, or a membership area? There's very likely a plugin for it. This modular approach lets you add exactly the functionality your site needs without paying a developer to build it from scratch, and swap it out later if your needs change.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://old.canadianwebdesigns.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/WordPress-for-E-commerce-1.png" alt="WordPress dashboard showing WooCommerce e-commerce plugin integration" class="wp-image-12481"/></figure>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-media">5. Built-in support for rich media</h2>
<p>WordPress natively handles text, images, audio, and video without extra configuration, which makes it straightforward to build media-rich pages — product galleries, video testimonials, embedded audio — without hunting for a plugin just to display content properly.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-scalability">6. It scales from a single page to a full catalog</h2>
<p>The same platform that runs a personal blog can run a multi-thousand-page lead generation site or online store. As your content and traffic grow, WordPress scales with the right hosting and caching setup, so you're not forced into a costly platform migration later.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-ecommerce">7. E-commerce is a plugin away, not a rebuild</h2>
<p>WooCommerce turns a standard WordPress site into a full online store — products, cart, checkout, shipping and payment processing — without switching platforms. That's a meaningful advantage over closed builders where adding a store often means an entirely different pricing tier or template system.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-seo">8. It's built with SEO in mind — but not automatically optimized</h2>
<p>WordPress produces clean, crawlable HTML, and plugins like Yoast or RankMath handle meta tags, sitemaps, and schema markup with minimal setup. That said, SEO plugins only manage the technical basics — <a href="/services/seo">actual search rankings</a> still depend on content quality, site speed, and backlinks.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-talent-pool">9. You're never stuck finding help</h2>
<p>WordPress powers such a large share of the web that finding a developer, designer, or agency who knows the platform is rarely difficult — and switching providers doesn't mean starting over, since the underlying system is the same everywhere.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-ease-of-use">10. A dashboard non-technical owners can actually use</h2>
<p>WordPress's admin dashboard is built for people who aren't developers — publishing a post, swapping an image, or updating a price doesn't require touching code. That lowers the cost of day-to-day content updates once the initial build is done.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">When WordPress Isn't the Right Call</h3>
<p>WordPress isn't automatic SEO or automatic security — an unmaintained install with outdated plugins is a common source of hacked sites. If you want a single static page with zero ongoing upkeep, a simpler builder may genuinely serve you better. For everything else — a business site that needs to grow, rank, and be owned outright — WordPress remains the stronger long-term bet.</p>
