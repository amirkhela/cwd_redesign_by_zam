<p>You can publish great content and still be invisible on Google. When that happens, the problem is usually <strong>technical SEO</strong> — the behind-the-scenes foundation that determines whether search engines can find, read, and trust your website. This guide explains what technical SEO actually is, why it quietly decides your rankings before content even enters the picture, and gives you a checklist any Canadian business owner can act on this week.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Technical SEO in One Sentence</h3>
<p>Technical SEO is everything that helps search engines <em>crawl</em> your site (discover your pages), <em>index</em> it (store those pages in Google's database), and <em>rank</em> it confidently (trust that the page will load fast and work on any device). Content tells Google what you're about; technical SEO determines whether Google ever gets the message. It's one of the three pillars of <a href="/blog/white-hat-seo-strategies-ethical-techniques-to-boost-your-website">sustainable, white hat SEO</a>, alongside content and links.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">The Four Pillars of Technical SEO</h3>
<p><strong>1. Crawlability.</strong> Googlebot discovers your site by following links and reading your XML sitemap. If your robots.txt accidentally blocks important pages, or your internal linking leaves pages orphaned with no path to them, those pages effectively don't exist. Every site should have a clean sitemap submitted in Google Search Console and internal links connecting related pages.</p>
<p><strong>2. Indexability.</strong> Crawled doesn't mean indexed. Google skips pages it considers thin, duplicated, or low-value — the dreaded "Crawled — currently not indexed" status in Search Console. Canonical tags, unique titles and descriptions, and substantial content on every page tell Google each URL deserves a spot in the index.</p>
<p><strong>3. Performance.</strong> Google's Core Web Vitals measure how fast your page loads (LCP), how quickly it responds to taps (INP), and how much the layout jumps around (CLS). Sites that pass rank more easily — and convert better. Most Canadian small-business sites we audit fail on oversized images and bloated plugins alone, which is also why <a href="/blog/toronto-website-maintenance-seo-optimization-keeping-your-site-fast-secure-and-google-ready">ongoing website maintenance</a> is an SEO activity, not just housekeeping.</p>
<p><strong>4. Mobile-friendliness and security.</strong> Google indexes the mobile version of your site first. If menus break or text is unreadable on a phone, rankings suffer everywhere. HTTPS is equally non-negotiable — browsers flag non-secure sites before visitors even see them.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What About Schema Markup?</h3>
<p>Schema (structured data) is code that labels your content so Google understands it precisely — this is a business, this is its address, these are FAQs, this is a review score. It powers the star ratings, FAQ dropdowns, and business panels you see in search results. For local Canadian businesses, LocalBusiness and FAQ schema are the highest-impact additions: they don't directly raise rankings, but they make your listing larger and more clickable, which raises traffic from the positions you already hold.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">A 10-Point Technical SEO Checklist You Can Run This Week</h3>
<p>1. Search <em>site:yourdomain.ca</em> on Google — are your important pages listed? 2. Load your homepage on your phone over cellular data — under 3 seconds? 3. Run your site through PageSpeed Insights and note the mobile score. 4. Check that yourdomain.ca/sitemap.xml exists and is submitted in Search Console. 5. Confirm robots.txt isn't blocking real pages. 6. Verify every page has HTTPS with no "not secure" warning. 7. Look for duplicate page titles in Search Console's indexing report. 8. Click every menu link — any 404s? 9. Confirm your phone number and address are clickable and consistent on every page. 10. Test your contact form — broken forms are the most expensive technical failure of all.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">When to Fix It Yourself vs. Call a Professional</h3>
<p>Items like fixing broken links, compressing images, and submitting a sitemap are absolutely DIY-able. But structural problems — index bloat, redirect chains, Core Web Vitals failures, schema implementation — usually live in your site's code and theme, where a wrong move can take pages offline. That's where a professional <a href="/services/seo">SEO service</a> earns its fee, and why technical SEO is built into every site our <a href="/services/web-design-development">web design and development team</a> ships: it's far cheaper to build the foundation right than to excavate it later.</p>
