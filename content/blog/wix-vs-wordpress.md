<p>Pick Wix if you want a simple site you can edit yourself this week and you are happy to stay on Wix's platform. Pick WordPress if you want to own the site outright, grow past a handful of pages or sell seriously online. Both can rank on Google. The difference is control, and how much of the work you want to do yourself.</p>

<p>We build on both, so this is not a sales pitch for one. Here is how they compare for a Canadian small business.</p>

<h2>When Wix is the right choice</h2>

<ul>
<li>You want to make your own changes without calling anyone.</li>
<li>The site is mostly information: services, hours, a contact form, a gallery.</li>
<li>You do not want to think about hosting, updates or security.</li>
<li>You expect the site to stay under about 20 pages.</li>
</ul>

<p>Wix has improved a lot. It handles mobile layouts, SSL and basic SEO settings out of the box, and Wix Studio gives designers more control. If you go this way, our <a href="/blog/wix-seo-setup-guide">Wix SEO setup guide</a> covers the settings worth changing.</p>

<h2>When WordPress is the right choice</h2>

<ul>
<li>You want to own the site and be free to change hosting or developers.</li>
<li>You plan to publish regularly, add service and location pages, or run a real blog.</li>
<li>You need features a builder does not offer: custom booking, member areas, integrations with your other software.</li>
<li>You want to sell online with a larger catalogue (WooCommerce).</li>
</ul>

<p>The trade-off is upkeep. WordPress needs core, theme and plugin updates and backups. If nobody does that, the site slows down and becomes a security risk. That is the job our <a href="/maintenance">maintenance plans</a> cover.</p>

<h2>Cost in Canada</h2>

<p>On Wix you pay one plan that includes hosting. On WordPress you pay a hosting company, plus any premium plugins. In both cases you also pay for a domain, and in both cases a professional design is a separate, one-time cost.</p>

<p>The build itself costs about the same either way. Our packages start at $299 for a site of up to 3 pages and $449 for a WordPress site you can edit yourself, plus HST. See <a href="/web-design-packages">all packages</a>.</p>

<h2>SEO: can a Wix site rank?</h2>

<p>Yes. Google does not rank platforms, it ranks pages. Wix gives you editable titles, descriptions, headings and a sitemap. WordPress gives you all of that plus finer control over technical details such as page speed, schema markup and redirects. For a small local business the difference rarely decides rankings. For a site with hundreds of pages, it starts to matter.</p>

<h2>Ecommerce</h2>

<p>Wix Stores works for a small shop with a few dozen products. For a larger catalogue, custom shipping rules or wholesale pricing, WooCommerce on WordPress gives you more room. If you are weighing a dedicated store platform instead, our <a href="/services/shopify-website-design">Shopify website design</a> page explains when Shopify is the better fit, and <a href="/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce">Shopify vs WooCommerce</a> compares the two side by side.</p>

<h2>Moving from Wix to WordPress</h2>

<p>It is a rebuild, not an export. The pages, text and images move across; the Wix design does not. The important part is setting up redirects from every old Wix URL to its new address so you keep the Google rankings you already have. We do this as part of a <a href="/services/wix-website-design">Wix to WordPress move</a>, and our <a href="/wix-alternative">Wix alternative</a> page covers what changes for you day to day.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Under 10 pages, you want to edit it yourself, no plans to grow:</strong> Wix.</li>
