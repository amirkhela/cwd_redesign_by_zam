<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Google Ads management isn't setting a campaign live and letting it run — it's the ongoing work of refining keywords, adjusting bids, testing ad copy, and tracking conversions so your budget actually turns into leads. Most wasted ad spend comes from campaigns nobody is actively managing after launch.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-is-google-ads">What Google Ads Actually Does</h2>
<p>Google Ads is Google's <a href="/blog/why-you-need-pay-per-click-like-google-ads">pay-per-click</a> advertising platform — you bid on keywords, write ads, and only pay when someone clicks. Ads can appear in search results, on partner sites through the Display Network, on YouTube, in Gmail, and in Google Shopping. The reach is real, but reach alone doesn't produce results — that's where management comes in.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-management-covers">What Real Google Ads Management Covers</h2>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Keyword and match-type strategy:</strong> broad match without a solid negative keyword list is the single biggest source of wasted spend — it pulls in searches that are related but not actually buying intent.</li>
<li><strong>Negative keywords:</strong> an ongoing list of terms you actively exclude, built from search-term reports showing what people typed to trigger your ad. This is maintenance, not a one-time setup.</li>
<li><strong>Bid and budget management:</strong> shifting spend toward keywords and audiences that convert, and away from ones that just burn budget — done well, this is a weekly task, not quarterly.</li>
<li><strong>Ad copy testing:</strong> running multiple ad variations against each other and keeping the ones with better click-through and conversion rates.</li>
<li><strong>Landing page alignment:</strong> an ad promising one thing that lands on a page about something else tanks Quality Score and conversion rate alike. See our guide on <a href="/blog/what-is-a-landing-page">what makes a landing page actually convert</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Conversion tracking:</strong> without tracking actual leads or sales (not just clicks), you're optimizing blind. This is the step most self-managed accounts skip entirely.</li>
<li><strong>Reporting and iteration:</strong> regular review of what's working, with adjustments based on data rather than "it feels like this campaign should work."</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-common-mistakes">Where Ad Spend Actually Gets Wasted</h2>
<p>The same handful of mistakes show up in almost every underperforming account we review:</p>
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Broad-match keywords with no negative keyword list, pulling in clicks with no buying intent.</li>
<li>No conversion tracking, so there's no real signal for which keywords or ads are actually working.</li>
<li>A generic homepage as the landing page instead of one built around the specific ad and offer.</li>
<li>A campaign launched once and left untouched — Quality Score and costs both drift the wrong way without active management.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-getting-started">Getting Started with Google Ads</h2>
<p>Set clear campaign goals before touching keywords — leads, sales, or brand awareness each call for a different campaign structure and bidding strategy. Start with a budget large enough to gather meaningful conversion data (roughly 15-20 conversions per campaign) before deciding whether to scale it up or shut it down. And pair it with the fundamentals: complementary channels like <a href="/blog/kijiji-ads-marketing-strategy-for-local-businesses">local marketplace ads</a> and <a href="/blog/local-seo-and-gmb-optimization">local SEO and Google Business Profile optimization</a> compound the effect of paid traffic rather than competing with it.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-conclusion">Should You Manage It Yourself or Hire It Out?</h2>
<p>Managing a small budget yourself is reasonable — the platform's interface is genuinely usable. The case for professional management grows with your spend: an agency's real job is catching the mistakes above before they burn through budget, which usually costs less than the wasted spend it prevents.</p>
