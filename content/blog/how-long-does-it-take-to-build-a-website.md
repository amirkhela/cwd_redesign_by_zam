Custom web applications are scheduled project by project. The biggest factor is not the design or the code; it is how quickly the text, photos and logins arrive.</p>

<h2>The five stages</h2>

<h3>1. Scope</h3>

<p>We agree on the pages, features and goals in writing. You send your logo, brand colours and any sites you like.</p>

<h3>2. Design</h3>

<p>You see the first mockup within 48 hours of sending content. Most projects need one or two rounds of changes.</p>

<h3>3. Build</h3>

<p>The approved design becomes a working site: pages, forms, booking or checkout, mobile layouts and on-page SEO.</p>

<h3>4. Content and review</h3>

<p>Your text and photos go in, and you review the whole site on your phone and computer. This is where most delays happen.</p>

<h3>5. Launch</h3>

<p>We connect the domain, set up SSL, submit the sitemap to Google and test every form. If you are replacing an old site, we set up redirects so you keep your search rankings; our <a href="/blog/website-migration-seo-checklist">migration checklist</a> explains why that matters.</p>

<h2>What slows a website down</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Content that is not ready.</strong> Pages cannot be finished without text and photos.</li>
<li><strong>Waiting on logins.</strong> Domain, hosting and old-site access often take days to find.</li>
<li><strong>Many decision makers.</strong> Each extra approver adds a review round.</li>
<li><strong>Scope changes mid-build.</strong> A new section is fine; a new direction restarts the design stage.</li>
<li><strong>Ecommerce data.</strong> Product names, prices, photos and shipping rules for every product take time to gather.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to get your website live faster</h2>

<ol>
<li>Write the text for your main pages before the project starts, even roughly.</li>
<li>Gather 10 to 20 real photos of your work, team or premises.</li>
<li>Find your domain and hosting logins on day one.</li>
<li>Name one person who approves the design.</li>
<li>Keep a list of "later" ideas instead of adding them mid-build.</li>
</ol>
