<p><strong>Short answer:</strong> sign in to your Google Business Profile, click <strong>Get verified</strong> and use the method Google offers you. In 2026 that is usually a <strong>short video</strong> recorded on your phone. Google may also offer phone or text, email, a live video call or a postcard. After you submit, Google says the review takes <strong>up to 5 business days</strong>.</p>

<p>Most failed verifications come from a video that misses one required item. This guide covers each method, exactly what to film and how to fix a failed attempt. Everything here is based on Google's Business Profile Help pages as of November 2026.</p>

<h2>Why verification matters</h2>

<p>An unverified profile does not show up properly in Google Maps or the local pack, and you cannot fully manage it: no reply to reviews, no posts, no performance data. If someone searches "plumber near me" or "web designer Mississauga", only verified businesses compete for those spots. See our guide on <a href="/blog/how-to-rank-in-the-google-map-pack">how to rank in the Google Map Pack</a> for what comes after verification.</p>

<p>If video is offered, Google recommends it, and in our experience it is the method most small businesses now get.</p>

<h2>How to record a verification video that passes</h2>

<p>Google needs three things from the video: <strong>where</strong> you are, <strong>proof the business exists</strong> and <strong>proof you manage it</strong>. Plan the shot before you press record, because you cannot pause or edit.</p>

<h3>For a storefront or office</h3>

<ol>
<li><strong>Start outside.</strong> Show the street sign, the building number or a nearby landmark.</li>
<li><strong>Show your signage.</strong> The business name on the sign must match the name on your profile.</li>
<li><strong>Go inside and prove you run it.</strong> Google's examples include opening the cash register, the kitchen or a storage room, or using your point-of-sale system.</li>
</ol>

<h3>For a service-area business (trades, mobile services)</h3>

<ol>
<li><strong>Show where you operate:</strong> street signs or landmarks in your area.</li>
<li><strong>Show the business exists:</strong> your tools, equipment or branded clothing.</li>
<li><strong>Show you manage it:</strong> unlock your branded van, or show a business permit, invoice or utility bill with the same name as your profile.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Video rules</h3>

<ul>
<li>Record live from the Business Profile on a phone. Pre-recorded uploads are not accepted.</li>
<li>One continuous take, at least 30 seconds, no edits or breaks.</li>
<li>Do not show bank, tax or ID numbers, private information, or other people's faces.</li>
</ul>

<h2>If your verification fails</h2>

<p>When a video is rejected, Google shows a <strong>Review issues</strong> notice with the reason. Common ones, in Google's own examples, are "business name not visible on storefront", "no nearby area shown" and "missing proof of authorization to operate the business". Fix that one item and record again.</p>

<p>If the <strong>Get verified</strong> button comes back after you thought you were done, Google could not fully verify the business and you need to try again.</p>

<p>Other fixes that solve most problems:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Match your name exactly.</strong> The profile name should match your signage and documents. Do not add keywords or city names to it.</li>
<li><strong>Do not edit while you wait.</strong> Changing the name, address or category while a postcard is on the way makes the code useless. Requesting a new postcard cancels the one in the mail.</li>
<li><strong>Use a direct phone line.</strong> If your number goes to a phone menu, the code will not reach you.</li>
<li><strong>Service-area business at home?</strong> Hide your address. In the profile go to Edit profile, then Location, then Business location, and turn off "Show business address to customers". You can list up to 20 service areas.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why Google asks you to verify again</h2>

<p>Google says a request to re-verify usually means "some of your business details were recently updated". Changing your name, address or main category can trigger it. Make big changes one at a time, and keep your website, signage and directory listings matching what is on the profile.</p>

<p>Google can also remove ownership from accounts that have not signed in for a long time. Log in every few weeks, reply to reviews and post updates.</p>

<h2>Watch out for verification scams</h2>

<p>Google says it will never ask for a one-time password or PIN and will never ask you to pay to keep your profile, get it verified or get it reinstated. If someone calls "from Google" offering paid verification, hang up.</p>
